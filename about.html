I'm a Computer Engineering student at Georgia Tech, and I build technology to solve real problems for the people and communities who need it.

I believe engineering is at its best when it makes society work better: when it makes healthcare more accessible, makes environments safer, and helps people live more independently. That belief guides which problems I take on and how I approach them. I start with the need, then build the system that meets it, whether that takes machine learning, embedded hardware, full-stack software, or a combination of all three.

Technology for health and safety

Much of my work has focused on healthcare. I developed an eye disease detection app that uses machine learning to screen for cataracts, glaucoma, and diabetic retinopathy with 96% accuracy. I validated it with 15 patients alongside an ophthalmologist, and it placed third in the Congressional App Challenge. Early screening tools like this can catch preventable vision loss in places where specialists are hard to reach.

What I'm doing now

I'm completing my B.S. in Computer Engineering at Georgia Tech on an accelerated three-year track, with a 4.0 GPA and graduation planned for May 2028. My coursework spans data structures and algorithms, digital system design, linear algebra, and differential equations. I'm focused on the intersection of intelligent software and real-world hardware, including embedded AI, robotics, and edge computing, where I believe the next generation of socially impactful technology will be built.
